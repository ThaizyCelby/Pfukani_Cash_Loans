The three documents

What we need, and what counts

01

Valid South African ID

Required

A clear copy or photo of your green barcoded ID book (both the photo page and the barcode page) or your smart ID card (front and back). The document must be valid not expired and all four corners must be visible.

• Green barcoded book, smart ID card, or a certified copy.
• Expired ID, blurry photo, or only one side of the smart card.
02

Latest Payslip

Required

Your most recent official payslip showing your gross salary, deductions, and net pay. The payslip must clearly show your employer's name, your name, and the pay period usually the current month or the immediately preceding one.

• Recent payslip (last 30 days), or 3 consecutive payslips for stability.
• Payslips older than 3 months, or handwritten or unsigned payslips.
Self-employed, commission-based, or grant recipient? No payslip is fine we accept alternative proof of income. See the alternatives section below.
03

6 Months Bank Statements

Required

Official PDF bank statements covering the last 6 months (180 days) of your primary transactional account. The statements must show your salary or regular income deposits, your name, and the account number. Every page must be included.

• Official PDF statements downloaded from your banking app or branch.
• Continuous, consecutive months no gaps.
• Screenshots, missing pages, or an account with no income deposits.

Alternative documents

If you don't receive a traditional payslip, we may accept one of the following talk to us first so we can confirm what applies to your situation.

Self-employed

Business bank statements, invoices, or a letter from your accountant confirming income.

Commission earners

3 months of commission statements alongside your latest payslip.

Grant / pension recipients

Your SASSA or pension letter plus bank statements showing the monthly deposits.

Contract / casual workers

Employment contract and bank statements demonstrating the regular deposit.

What not to send

For your own security, never send us these we will never ask for them:

  • •Your bank card PIN or online banking password
  • •Your physical bank card or ID book we never keep originals
  • •One-time PINs (OTPs) received on your phone
  • •Photos of documents you don't recognise or can't verify

Tips for faster verification

1

Photograph flat, well-lit, uncropped. Place documents on a flat surface with good light, and make sure all four corners are in the frame.

2

Send the PDF bank statements not screenshots. Download statements directly from your banking app so all six months are included and every page is legible.

3

Label each file clearly. Name files with your surname and the document type for example, "Mokoena-ID.pdf" or "Mokoena-Payslip-Jan.pdf".

4

Send everything in one message. Bundle all three documents in a single WhatsApp or email thread so we can process them together.

Common reasons for document rejection

Blurry or unreadable text and numbers must be clear.

Cropped corners the whole document must be visible.

Missing months bank statements must cover 6 continuous months.

Expired ID renew before applying.

Wrong account statements must be your primary salary account.

Altered documents any tampering ends the application.

Quick answers

Do you need certified copies?

For most loans, clear photos or digital copies are enough. Certified copies may occasionally be requested for specific verifications we'll tell you upfront if that applies.

Can I submit documents from a joint account?

Yes, provided your name appears on the statements and the income deposit is clearly visible. We may also ask for the other account holder's consent or additional verification.

How long do you keep my documents?

Enquiries that don't result in a loan: up to 12 months. Credit agreements: at least 5 years after settlement, as required by the NCA. See the Privacy Policy for full retention periods.

What if my documents are in a different name or language?

Documents must be in your legal name. If they're in a language other than English or Afrikaans, you may need to provide a sworn translation. Talk to us first so we can advise.
